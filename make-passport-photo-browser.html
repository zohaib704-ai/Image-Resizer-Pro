Make a passport photo in your browser

No app, no upload, no subscription. Crop, resize, and export an official-dimension passport photo in five minutes.

Dec 20, 2025 4 min read By the editors

Standard passport photo dimensions

Requirements vary by country, but these cover the most common:

  • United States: 2 × 2 inches (51 × 51 mm), 600 × 600 pixels at 300 DPI.
  • United Kingdom: 35 × 45 mm, 413 × 531 pixels at 300 DPI.
  • European Union (Schengen): 35 × 45 mm, same as UK.
  • India: 51 × 51 mm (2 × 2 inch) or 35 × 45 mm depending on document.
  • Canada: 50 × 70 mm.
  • Pakistan: 35 × 45 mm (passport), 25 × 30 mm (visa) — always double-check the specific form.

Whatever the destination, the workflow is identical: crop to the right ratio, resize to the pixel dimensions, save as JPEG under the file-size cap.

Step-by-step

  1. Take a good source photo. Plain background, even lighting, no shadows, neutral expression, no glasses. Phone cameras are fine — you just need to shoot straight on.
  2. Upload it to ImageResizer Pro.
  3. Crop with the locked ratio (4:5 matches 35 × 45 mm; 1:1 matches 2 × 2 inch). Position the head so it fills roughly 70–80% of the frame.
  4. Apply Crop.
  5. Resize to the exact pixel dimensions the form requires (e.g. 600 × 600 for US).
  6. Compress to JPEG at 85% quality — most forms cap uploads at 200–500 KB.
  7. Download.

Getting the crop right

Passport photo standards usually require:

  • Head centred, looking straight at the camera.
  • Eyes within a specific zone — typically the upper third of the image.
  • Head height filling 50–70% of the frame height.
  • Some margin above the head — usually 3–5 mm for UK/EU, more for US.

The crop handles let you fine-tune. Start with the default (auto-crop area 85%) then adjust the corners until the head sits comfortably.

Background: the silent killer

Most rejections happen because of the background, not the face. Use a plain white, off-white, or light grey wall. Avoid shadows behind the head, and don't use a wall with a picture frame or switch plate visible. If your original photo has a busy background, either reshoot or use a background-removal tool — though most passport offices reject heavily edited photos, so reshoot if you can.

What NOT to do

  • Don't over-sharpen. Most passport rules forbid cosmetic retouching. Enhance lightly if at all.
  • Don't crop too tight. Most rejections for "head too large" come from cropping without margin above the hair.
  • Don't use a filter. Even a mild sepia will fail at most offices.
  • Don't submit PNG. Most forms only accept JPEG.

Double-check with the official source

Every country's passport office publishes a PDF spec. Before you submit, look up the current requirements for your specific document — they change, and the exact pixel dimensions and file-size cap matter. The steps above are correct for the dimensions listed; the paperwork is your responsibility.
