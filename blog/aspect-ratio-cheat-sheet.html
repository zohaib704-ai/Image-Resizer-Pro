Aspect ratio cheat sheet

Every ratio you'll ever need, what it's used for, and the exact pixel dimensions for each major platform.

Dec 12, 2025 5 min read By the editors

What "aspect ratio" actually means

An aspect ratio describes the shape of an image: the relationship between its width and height. It's written as W:H, always reduced. A 1920 × 1080 photo is 16:9. A 1080 × 1080 photo is 1:1 (or 1).

The reason it matters: every platform has an ideal shape for every slot. Crop to the wrong shape and your image either gets letterboxed, cropped automatically (often badly), or looks out of place.

The core ratios

1:1 — Square

Instagram feed posts, profile pictures, product photos, avatars. The most neutral ratio; works everywhere, rarely fails.

Pixel sizes: 1080 × 1080 (Instagram), 400 × 400 (avatars), 2000 × 2000 (high-res product).

4:5 — Portrait

Instagram portrait posts, Pinterest pins, print photographs. The ratio is just tall enough to feel "portrait" without dominating a feed. Currently Instagram's most engaging ratio because it occupies more vertical space than a square.

Pixel sizes: 1080 × 1350 (Instagram portrait), 800 × 1000 (standard).

16:9 — Widescreen

The default for video and YouTube thumbnails. Also used for hero images on websites and desktop wallpapers.

Pixel sizes: 1920 × 1080 (Full HD), 1280 × 720 (HD), 3840 × 2160 (4K).

9:16 — Vertical

Stories, Reels, TikTok, Shorts, mobile-first content. The exact inverse of 16:9 — same total pixels, just rotated.

Pixel sizes: 1080 × 1920 (standard), 720 × 1280 (compact).

4:3 — Classic

Traditional photo print, most point-and-shoot cameras, some older displays. Still common in phone camera sensors and digital photo frames.

Pixel sizes: 1600 × 1200, 4000 × 3000, 800 × 600.

3:2 — DSLR standard

35mm film and most DSLR/mirrorless sensors. Common in professional print photography and photo book layouts.

Pixel sizes: 3000 × 2000, 6000 × 4000, 1500 × 1000.

Platform-specific sizes (2026)

  • Instagram feed: 1080 × 1080 (square) or 1080 × 1350 (portrait).
  • Instagram Stories / Reels: 1080 × 1920.
  • Facebook feed: 1200 × 630 (1.91:1).
  • Twitter/X: 1600 × 900 (16:9).
  • LinkedIn: 1200 × 627 (1.91:1).
  • YouTube thumbnail: 1280 × 720 (16:9).
  • Pinterest: 1000 × 1500 (2:3).
  • Open Graph (link previews): 1200 × 630 (1.91:1).

How to crop without losing the subject

If you're cropping a 16:9 photo down to 1:1, you'll lose the sides. Two strategies:

  1. Shoot with margin. When in doubt, leave extra space around your subject so you can crop later. Better to have too much frame than too little.
  2. Centre the subject. A centred subject survives almost any crop. Off-centre subjects need careful re-framing per platform.

Use the Crop tool in this app to preview different ratios before committing. The handles are draggable, and the ratio selector locks the aspect so you can't accidentally squish the image.

One rule of thumb

If you can only remember one thing: 1:1 is universal, 4:5 is Instagram, 16:9 is video, 9:16 is mobile-first. Those four cover about 95% of use cases.
