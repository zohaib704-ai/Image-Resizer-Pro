JPEG vs PNG vs WebP: which one should you use?

Three formats. Three different jobs. Pick the wrong one and your page loads slower for no visual gain.

Jan 8, 2026 5 min read By the editors

The short answer

  • Photos → JPEG or WebP.
  • Logos, icons, screenshots with text → PNG.
  • Photos on a modern site → WebP (best of both).

If that's all you need, stop reading. The rest is the reasoning behind those rules — useful when you're deciding case-by-case.

JPEG: the photo workhorse

JPEG has been the default for photographs since the early '90s. It uses lossy compression — meaning it discards visual information the eye is unlikely to notice. At 75–85% quality, a photo is typically indistinguishable from the original while being 5–15× smaller.

Strengths: tiny files for photos, universal support, adjustable quality.

Weaknesses: no transparency, gets ugly fast at low quality, terrible for text (edges smear).

PNG: the crisp lossless format

PNG uses lossless compression — every pixel is preserved exactly. That makes it perfect for anything with sharp edges, flat colours, or transparency: logos, charts, screenshots of UIs, images with text.

Strengths: pixel-perfect, supports transparency, excellent for graphics.

Weaknesses: photos become huge (2–5× the size of equivalent JPEG). No way to "compress more" without losing quality.

A common mistake: people screenshot a photo and save it as PNG. That's a 3 MB file where a 200 KB JPEG would look identical on screen.

WebP: the modern default

WebP was developed by Google and released in 2010. It supports both lossy and lossless compression, plus transparency — so it can replace JPEG and PNG for most use cases. It typically produces files 25–35% smaller than JPEG at the same visual quality.

Strengths: smaller files than both JPEG and PNG, supports transparency, supports animation.

Weaknesses: slightly less universal than JPEG (older software may reject it), some CMS platforms still don't accept it as an upload format.

Every modern browser supports WebP — Chrome, Firefox, Safari (14+), Edge. As of 2026 it's safe to serve it.

The decision tree

  1. Is it a photograph or a photorealistic image?
    • Yes → use WebP if the platform accepts it, otherwise JPEG at 80%.
    • No, and it has transparency or sharp text → use PNG.
  2. Does the destination site or form reject the format?
    • Yes → fall back to JPEG.
  3. Are you optimizing for page speed?
    • Yes → use WebP.

The trap: converting between formats badly

Converting a JPEG to PNG makes the file bigger without making it look better — the detail PNG preserves was already lost when the JPEG was first created. Same with JPEG → WebP: you can't recover quality that's already gone.

The correct direction is always: start from the original, then export once to the format you need.

What about AVIF?

AVIF is the newest format (2019) and compresses 20–50% better than WebP. Browser support is now universal as of 2025, but encoding is slower and some platforms still don't accept it. It's a future-proof choice, but WebP remains the safest "modern" default for now.
