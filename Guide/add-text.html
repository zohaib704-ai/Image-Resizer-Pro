How to add text to a photo

Captions, watermarks, titles, and memes — with full control over size, colour, and position.

Jan 9, 2026 4 min read By the editors

What this tool does

The Add Text tool overlays a single line of text on your image, with a black outline stroke for readability. You control the text content, size (12–200px), colour, and X/Y position (both as percentages of the image). The preview updates live; click Apply Text to bake it permanently into the image.

When to use it

  • Adding a caption to a photo for social media.
  • Watermarking your own images before sharing.
  • Making memes or quote cards.
  • Labelling a scanned photo for an archive or family album.

Step-by-step

  1. Upload your image and open the Add Text tool.
  2. Type your caption in the text field.
  3. Adjust Size until the text is comfortably readable. 48px is a good starting point.
  4. Pick a Colour — white and yellow work on most photos because of the black outline stroke.
  5. Position with X and Y. X is horizontal (0% left, 100% right), Y is vertical (0% top, 100% bottom).
  6. Click Apply Text to commit.

Tips for best results

  • White or yellow text on a busy photo. The black outline makes both readable on almost any background.
  • Y = 85% (near the bottom) is the classic caption position — visible but doesn't cover the subject.
  • Size 40–60px is right for most photos up to 2000px wide. On very large images, push the size up proportionally.
  • Add text after Enhance. Enhancing first gives the text a clean base to sit on.

Common mistakes

  • Text at the very edge. Keep X between 10–90% and Y between 10–90%. Text at 0% or 100% gets clipped.
  • Dark text on a dark background. Even with the outline, black on black disappears. Use white on dark images.
  • Forgetting to apply. The preview is not the final image. Click Apply Text to bake it in before downloading.
  • Adding multiple texts. The tool applies one caption at a time. To add more, apply the first, then type new text and apply again.
