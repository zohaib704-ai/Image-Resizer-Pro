How to send a photo to Google Lens

Download first, then hand off cleanly — the only way to use Lens without a direct upload box.

Jan 6, 2026 2 min read By the editors

What this tool does

Google Lens is a powerful reverse-image search and OCR service, but it doesn't accept direct links or embedded images — it needs a file. This tool bridges that gap in two clean steps: download the current image with a sensible filename, then open Google Lens in a new tab where you upload it.

When to use it

  • Reverse image search — find where an image came from or what it depicts.
  • Translate text in a sign, menu, or document you photographed.
  • Identify a product, plant, or landmark from a photo.
  • Extract text from an image using Google's stronger OCR (for difficult fonts).

Step-by-step

  1. Upload your image and open the Google Lens tool.
  2. Click 1. Download Current Image. It saves to your device as yourfilename-for-lens.png.
  3. Click 2. Open Google Lens. A new tab opens at lens.google.com/upload.
  4. On the Lens page, drag the downloaded file into the upload area, or click to browse and select it.
  5. Lens processes it and returns results.

Tips for best results

  • Know where your downloads go. On Windows it's Downloads; on macOS it's Downloads; on iOS it's the Files app; on Android it's the Files or Downloads folder.
  • For translation, use a clear, straight, well-lit photo. Lens's translation works much better on text it can read clearly.
  • Try OCR first. If all you need is text, the built-in Read Text (OCR) tool is faster and doesn't require leaving the site.
  • The filename is deliberate. "ImageResizer-for-lens.png" is easy to spot if you have hundreds of downloads — saves you hunting for it later.

Common mistakes

  • Pasting into Lens directly. Lens has no "paste image" input. You must upload a file.
  • Forgetting the download. If you skip step 1, there's nothing to upload on the Lens page.
  • Losing the file. If your Downloads folder is cluttered, the file can get lost. Open your most recent downloads first.
  • Expecting Lens to be private. Unlike every other tool here, Lens does upload your image to Google's servers. That's inherent to how it works. If privacy is essential, use the built-in OCR tool instead.
