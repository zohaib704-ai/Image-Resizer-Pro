How to compress a photo

Hit any size cap in JPEG, WebP, or PNG — and know which format to pick.

Jan 10, 2026 5 min read By the editors

What this tool does

The Compress tool re-encodes your image at a chosen quality level (10–100%) and format (JPEG, WebP, or PNG), then downloads the result. The quality slider trades file size against visual fidelity. The panel shows the resulting file size after compression, so you can iterate until you're under your target.

When to use it

  • Hitting an upload form's file-size cap (100 KB, 200 KB, 500 KB, 1 MB).
  • Reducing page weight for a website or newsletter.
  • Fitting more photos into an email attachment.
  • Preparing images to be archived at a smaller size.

Step-by-step

  1. Upload your image and open the Compress tool.
  2. Drag the Quality slider to a starting point. 80% is a safe default for JPEG.
  3. Pick a Format: JPEG for photos, WebP for smaller files at the same look, PNG only if you need transparency or flat-colour graphics.
  4. Click Compress & Download.
  5. Check the file size shown below the button. If it's over your target, drop the quality by 10% and try again.

Tips for best results

  • JPEG for photos. Always. PNG is 3–5× larger for photos with no visual gain.
  • WebP for speed. If the destination accepts WebP, it typically produces 25–35% smaller files than JPEG at the same quality.
  • 75–85% quality is the sweet spot. Below 65%, artefacts become visible around edges. Above 90%, you pay for quality the eye can't see.
  • Resize before compressing. A 3000px image compressed to 100 KB will look much worse than a 1200px image compressed to 100 KB. Smaller source = cleaner output at the same file size.

Common mistakes

  • Using PNG for photos. The single most common compression mistake. PNG is for logos, screenshots, and transparency — not photos.
  • Dropping quality below 50%. The file gets ugly fast. If you need a smaller size, resize first, then compress.
  • Re-compressing an already-compressed image. JPEG → JPEG again compounds artefacts. Always compress from the original.
  • Ignoring the file-size readout. The tool tells you exactly what you produced — use it to hit your target, not guess.
