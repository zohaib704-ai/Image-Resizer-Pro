How to use the Enhance tool

Six sliders that fix almost any photo — brightness, contrast, saturation, warmth, clarity, and denoise.

Jan 15, 2026 5 min read By the editors

What this tool does

The Enhance tool gives you live control over the six variables that determine how a photo looks: light (brightness), punch (contrast), colour intensity (saturation), colour temperature (warmth), sharpness (clarity), and grain reduction (denoise). Changes preview instantly on the canvas. When you're happy, click Apply to Image to bake them in permanently.

When to use it

  • Photos shot in low or uneven light that look grey or flat.
  • Scanned documents or printed photos that have yellowed with age.
  • Phone photos that look "washed out" compared to what you saw with your eyes.
  • Any image you're about to crop, resize, or compress — Enhance first, then everything else.

Step-by-step

  1. Upload your image on the homepage. The editor opens automatically.
  2. Click the Enhance tool card (or open it from the toolbar).
  3. Start with Contrast — drag it up to around 115–130% and watch the image "pop" into place.
  4. Adjust Brightness — up to 110–120% if the image is dark, down to 90–95% if it's blown out.
  5. Nudge Saturation — 105–115% for a richer look, 0% for black-and-white, or leave at 100% if colours already look accurate.
  6. Use Warmth if the image has a colour cast. Positive adds warmth (fixes bluish photos), negative cools (fixes yellow ones).
  7. Add Clarity at 30–50% to sharpen edges — the sweet spot for most photos.
  8. Only touch Denoise if the image has visible grain (high-ISO phone shots, scanned photos). 1–2 is usually enough.
  9. Click Apply to Image to commit. Or click Reset Values to start over.

Tips for best results

  • Contrast is the highest-leverage slider. If you only move one, move this one.
  • Clarity above 80% starts to look unnatural — halos appear around edges. Stay in the 30–50% band.
  • Denoise softens detail. It's a trade-off, not a free win. Keep it under 2 for photos, under 3 for scans.
  • Warmth is a corrective tool, not a style choice. Use it to fix colour casts, not to make photos "feel warmer" — that's what filters are for.

Common mistakes

  • Maxing out Saturation. Above 140% skin tones turn orange. Rarely what you want.
  • Applying Clarity above 80%. Creates visible halos and ringing around high-contrast edges.
  • Pushing Denoise above 3. Smoothes skin and text into mush. If you need more than 2, the source is probably too low quality.
  • Enhancing after compression. Compress sharpens artefacts. Enhance first, then compress.
