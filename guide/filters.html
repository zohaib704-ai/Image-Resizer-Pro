How to apply a filter

Ten one-tap presets that transform the whole photo in a click — from black-and-white to inverted.

Jan 14, 2026 4 min read By the editors

What this tool does

The Filters tool applies a pre-set colour grade to the entire image — a CSS filter string you can preview and then commit. Ten presets are available: Original, B&W, Sepia, Vivid, Cool, Warm, Fade, Drama, Invert, Blur. Each one is a one-tap transformation, perfect for social posts where you want a quick look without fiddling with sliders.

When to use it

  • You want a quick mood change — monochrome, sepia, or faded vintage.
  • You're making a social post and want a consistent look across multiple images.
  • You need to convert a photo to pure black-and-white without manually dropping saturation.
  • You want to fake an old photo, invert colours for a design, or soften a background with Blur.

Step-by-step

  1. Upload your image and open the Filters tool.
  2. Click any preset chip. The preview updates instantly on the canvas.
  3. Try three or four — there's no cost to comparing.
  4. When you've settled on one, click Apply to Image to bake it in.
  5. If you apply and want to change your mind, click Reset in the toolbar to reload the original upload.

Tips for best results

  • Enhance first, then filter. Applying a filter to a flat, underexposed photo locks in the problem. Fix the exposure first.
  • B&W is the safest choice. Black-and-white is forgiving — it hides colour casts and noise and looks deliberate.
  • Fade + Enhance is a common combo: apply Fade, then bump Contrast to 110% to keep it from looking washed out.
  • Blur is a background tool. Set it to 2px and it hides busy backgrounds; above 5px it just looks broken.

Common mistakes

  • Stacking filters. Apply → pick another → apply again — each one compounds. Commit once if you want a clean look.
  • Filtering after adding text. Text you've already baked in gets tinted. Apply the filter first, then add text.
  • Filtering a photo you plan to OCR. Filters like Sepia and Invert destroy OCR accuracy. Skip filters if you're reading text.
  • Invert for anything other than design. It's a useful effect but never produces a "natural" photo.
