How to read text from an image

Extract printed text from any photo in eight languages — entirely in your browser.

Jan 8, 2026 5 min read By the editors

What this tool does

The Read Text (OCR) tool uses Tesseract.js — an open-source OCR engine compiled to WebAssembly — to detect and extract printed text from your image. It supports eight languages: English, Urdu, Arabic, Hindi, French, Spanish, German, and Simplified Chinese. The first time you use a language, its model downloads once (~10–15 seconds); after that it's cached by your browser. Your image never leaves your device.

When to use it

  • Copy-pasting text from a screenshot, PDF screenshot, or phone photo.
  • Digitising a scanned receipt, invoice, or form.
  • Extracting text from a slide, poster, or menu you photographed.
  • Making a document searchable by converting it to text.

Step-by-step

  1. Upload your image and open Read Text (OCR).
  2. Pick the language of the text in the image. If it's mostly English, leave the default.
  3. Click Extract Text. The first run downloads the language model.
  4. Wait for progress to finish. Recognition takes a few seconds for most images.
  5. The extracted text appears in the box below. Click Copy Text to grab it.

Tips for best results

  • High resolution wins. 300 DPI scans or a full-resolution phone photo produce far more accurate text than a downscaled one.
  • Straight, not tilted. A 5° tilt noticeably cuts accuracy. Rotate before OCR.
  • High contrast. Use the Enhance tool to push Contrast to 130–150% before OCR. This alone often improves accuracy by 5–10%.
  • Crop tight. Remove logos, photos, and borders — the engine reads them as noise.
  • One language per image. Mixed-language pages reduce accuracy for both languages.

Common mistakes

  • Wrong language model. Running English text through the Urdu model produces gibberish. Always match the language.
  • Expecting handwriting. Tesseract is trained on printed text. Cursive handwriting is unreliable (30–60% accuracy at best).
  • OCR on a blurred or compressed image. Sharp edges are essential. If you must compress first, keep quality above 80%.
  • Reading multi-column layouts. Columns get interleaved. Crop each column separately and run OCR on each.
