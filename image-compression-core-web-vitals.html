Why image compression matters for Core Web Vitals

Images are often the single biggest item in a page's payload. Shrinking them is the highest-leverage speed fix available.

Dec 28, 2025 6 min read By the editors

Core Web Vitals, briefly

Google measures three things that reflect real user experience:

  • LCP (Largest Contentful Paint) — how fast the biggest visible element loads. Usually a hero image or a heading. Target: under 2.5 seconds.
  • INP (Interaction to Next Paint) — how responsive the page is to clicks and taps. Target: under 200 ms.
  • CLS (Cumulative Layout Shift) — how much the page jumps as it loads. Target: under 0.1.

Images touch two of these directly (LCP and CLS) and can influence the third indirectly. That's why image optimization is the highest-leverage performance work on most content sites.

Why images dominate LCP

On a typical blog post, article header, or product page, the largest visible element is a photograph — a hero image, a product shot, a featured article thumbnail. That image is the LCP element. If it takes 4 seconds to load, LCP is 4 seconds, no matter how fast everything else is.

Most hero images today are 800 KB – 2.5 MB. Loading that on a 3G connection takes 6–20 seconds. Compressing to 150–250 KB brings that down to 1–3 seconds — typically the difference between "passes" and "fails" on LCP.

The three image levers that matter

  1. Format. Serving WebP instead of JPEG saves 25–35%. Serving AVIF saves 40–50%. Same visual quality.
  2. Dimensions. A 2000 × 1500 image displayed at 600 × 450 on mobile is loading 4× more pixels than it can show. Serve a right-sized version per viewport.
  3. Compression level. A quality-85 JPEG is typically indistinguishable from a quality-95 one, but 30–40% smaller.

In practice: resize, compress, and pick a modern format. That single three-step pipeline usually cuts image weight by 70–90% with no perceptible quality loss.

Also worth doing

  • Width and height attributes. Always set them on <img> tags so the browser reserves layout space — this is the single biggest fix for CLS.
  • Lazy loading. Add loading="lazy" to below-the-fold images so they don't block LCP.
  • Responsive srcset. Serve different sizes per viewport. A phone doesn't need a 2000-pixel image.
  • CDN with auto-format. Cloudflare, Cloudinary, and Netlify's image CDN can auto-convert to WebP/AVIF per browser. That's the "set and forget" solution.

How much difference does it really make?

Real-world case studies tend to land in similar ranges:

  • 1 MB → 150 KB hero image: LCP drops 2–4 seconds on cable, 6–10 seconds on mobile 3G.
  • All images compressed 70%: total page weight often drops 50%+, improving INP slightly and CLS significantly.
  • Full pipeline (resize + compress + WebP): Lighthouse Performance score often jumps 20–40 points.

None of this requires paid tools. It's just applying the three levers consistently across every image on the page.

Do it once, do it right

The easiest way to stay ahead: compress images before uploading them to your CMS. Most CMSes don't re-compress at all — they store whatever you upload. So the size on your laptop becomes the size on your site.
